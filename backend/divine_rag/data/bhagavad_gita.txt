Bhagavad Gita Teachings:

Chapter 2, Verse 47:
You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions. Never consider yourself to be the cause of the results of your activities, nor be attached to inaction.

Chapter 2, Verse 48:
Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga.

Chapter 2, Verse 62:
While contemplating the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment for them, and from such attachment lust develops, and from lust anger arises.

Chapter 2, Verse 63:
From anger, complete delusion arises, and from delusion bewilderment of memory. When memory is bewildered, intelligence is lost, and when intelligence is lost one falls down again into the material pool.

Chapter 6, Verse 5:
One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well.

Chapter 6, Verse 6:
For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will be the greatest enemy.

Chapter 18, Verse 66:
Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear.

Summary of Teachings:
- Karma Yoga: Focus on action, not the results.
- Equanimity: Stay balanced in success and failure.
- Mind Control: Master your mind to make it your friend.
- Detachment: Avoid excessive attachment to material outcomes.
- Self-Knowledge: Realize your true self beyond the body and mind.
- Devotion: Surrender to the divine for peace and protection.
- Duty (Dharma): Follow your righteous path with discipline.
